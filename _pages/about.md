I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the University of Minnesota. My fields are **textual analysis and empirical macroeconomics**. I study when the text of news and research carries economic information that standard data miss, using large text corpora and machine-learning methods. **I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.**

Before Minnesota, I earned an M.Sc. in Economics from EIEF and LUISS (Rome) and a B.Sc. in Economics from Lomonosov Moscow State University. I teach undergraduate econometrics at Minnesota.

## Job Market Paper

[**When the News Matters: Newspaper Text and Real-Time Forecasts of U.S. Growth**](/files/Aleksandr_Shneider_JMP.pdf)

I build monthly text measures from 11.76 million articles in seven U.S. newspapers and ask whether, and when, they improve real-time forecasts of U.S. growth. The gains are concentrated in the Great Recession: in four quarters of 2008–09, text beats an autoregressive benchmark by 10–13% for GDP and 20–35% for consumption, while in normal times neither text nor hard data does.
